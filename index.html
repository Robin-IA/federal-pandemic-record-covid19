      What will you find here?

      The Federal Pandemic Record gathers COVID-19 documents that U.S. government agencies, like the CDC and the White House, put on their websites. The Wayback Machine saved copies of those pages. Each link opens a saved document, such as a fact sheet, a PDF or an executive order. The site also shows COVID-19 items from Democracy's Library, an Internet Archive curated collection of government resources.

      How documents are dated

      Most documents show the month they were first saved, like by March 2020. The real date may be a little earlier.

      Finding documents in other languages

      The CDC shared many documents in other languages, such as Spanish, Chinese and Vietnamese. To find them, type a language in the search box, like Spanish, or tick Has translations. Each result shows how many languages it comes in. For now, the link opens the English version.

      What happens when pages move or disappear?

      We have collected everything that we can, even when pages move or disappear! For example, when the CDC moved a page, the old address often stayed online to send visitors to the new page. On the Pages over time tab, those are shaded red, with a link to the new page.

      When President Biden took office on January 20, 2021, President Trump's White House website moved to a new address, trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov. Copies saved after the move show when the page moved, not when the document came out. So each Trump White House document is dated by the first time it was saved from the White House website, before the move.
